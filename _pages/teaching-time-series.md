**TU Berlin · PhD-level workshop · Two days**

I independently designed and taught this workshop around a practical question: how can we build a defensible empirical model when economic variables are persistent, relationships may change, and long-run information matters?

## Course outline

1. **Dynamic modelling.** Time indexing, lags, autoregressive and distributed-lag models, short- and long-run effects, and residual diagnostics.
2. **Non-stationarity and equilibrium correction.** Stochastic trends, unit-root tests, cointegration and the interpretation of adjustment towards a long-run relationship.
3. **Structural change.** Prespecified and unknown changes, indicator saturation, sensitivity and interpretation of selected breaks.
4. **Multivariate dynamics.** VAR specification, diagnostics, predictability, forecasts and impulse responses.
5. **Long-run systems and structural shocks.** VECMs, cointegration rank, restrictions, weak exogeneity and the identifying assumptions behind structural impulse responses.

## Learning outcomes

Participants practise choosing and diagnosing a dynamic model, interpreting equilibrium adjustment and assessing evidence of change. They examine when variables should be modelled jointly, and why a statistically adequate model still needs an identification argument before shocks or policy changes receive a causal interpretation.

## Format and preparation

The workshop combines explanations, R demonstrations, guided exercises and discussion of empirical choices. It assumes working knowledge of OLS and basic R. Exercises use controlled examples to separate known data-generating features from the conclusions that could be drawn in a substantive policy application. More advanced material provides follow-up study beyond the two-day core.
