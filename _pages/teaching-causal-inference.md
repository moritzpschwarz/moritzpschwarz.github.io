**TU Berlin · PhD-level workshop · Two days**

I independently designed and taught this workshop to connect identification arguments with practical empirical analysis. The course moves from the question being asked to the comparisons and assumptions needed to answer it.

## Course outline

1. **Causal questions and identification.** Potential outcomes, treatment effects, randomised experiments and quasi-experimental variation.
2. **Instrumental variables and difference-in-differences.** Identification assumptions, estimation, inference and threats to credible interpretation.
3. **Regression discontinuity.** Sharp and fuzzy designs, local comparisons and specification choices.
4. **Synthetic controls and related approaches.** Constructing counterfactuals, assessing fit, placebo comparisons and synthetic difference-in-differences.
5. **Extensions and critical interpretation.** Treatment heterogeneity, staggered adoption and the relationship between research design and implementation.

## Learning outcomes

Participants practise explaining an identification strategy, stating its assumptions and choosing diagnostics that address the main threats to a comparison. R examples and exercises connect estimation to interpretation: a coefficient alone does not establish a credible causal effect.

## Format and preparation

The workshop combines conceptual discussion, mathematical formulations, worked applications and practical exercises. Familiarity with regression and basic R is helpful. The emphasis is on informed use and critical assessment of methods, rather than a catalogue of estimators.
